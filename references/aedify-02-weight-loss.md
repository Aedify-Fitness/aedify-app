# Weight Loss 101

> Source: thefitness.wiki — The official wiki of r/Fitness and r/weightroom on Reddit.

## Core Principle

Weight loss is straightforward despite the illusion of complexity. Your body requires a certain number of calories each day to fuel its activities — this is your **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**.

- If you eat **more** than your TDEE (surplus) → you **gain** weight
- If you eat **fewer** than your TDEE (deficit) → you **lose** weight

All diets that work achieve weight loss through the same mechanism: manipulating calorie balance. You can lose weight through diet alone; exercise is not required for weight loss (though it is beneficial for overall health).

## Two Common Points of Failure

1. **Overestimating your TDEE**: No TDEE calculation is 100% accurate. Treat them as estimates. Exercise calorie burn calculations tend to be wildly inaccurate and inflated.
2. **Underestimating calorie consumption**: From unconscious snacking, ignoring calories in drinks or dressings, to simply not tracking at all.

**The scale doesn't lie.** If you are not losing weight, you are not in a true caloric deficit, regardless of what a calculator says. See also: [Why can't I lose weight?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/why-cant-i-lose-weight/)

For all practical purposes, weight loss and fat loss are interchangeable. If you are losing weight, you are necessarily losing fat.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Take "Before" Measurements
At minimum: weight and photos (front, back, side). This lets you compare progress over time.

### Step 2: Estimate Your TDEE
Use any online TDEE calculator as a starting point. Remember this is an **estimate only** — expect to adjust it.

### Step 3: Set a Daily Calorie Goal
Reduce your TDEE by **10-20%** (TDEE × 0.8 to 0.9). Avoid going below 20% under your TDEE. Going too low increases the risk of:
- Malnourishment
- Muscle loss
- Low energy
- Inadequate fat intake for hormonal balance
- Restriction-binge cycles

### Step 4: Track Your Calorie Consumption
Use tools like [MyFitnessPal](http://www.myfitnesspal.com/) or [NutritionData](http://nutritiondata.self.com/). Track **everything** you eat and drink, including additives and toppings. The [nSuns Adaptive TDEE Spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1QTWDxFaB0r-7U6vZmG1s8of7jwd2GHHi/edit) can adjust your estimated TDEE over time.

### Step 5: Take Regular Progress Measurements
- Weigh yourself once a day (or at least once a week), unclothed, on an empty stomach
- Don't fixate on day-to-day fluctuations — track the **trend over time**
- Consider monthly progress photos
- Avoid bio-electrical impedance body fat measurements (extremely inaccurate)

### Step 6: Adjust Your Diet Over Time
As you lose weight, your TDEE decreases (less mass = fewer calories needed). Your starting calorie goal will eventually stop causing weight loss. Adjust downward as needed.

### Step 7: Take Regular Maintenance Breaks
Renaissance Periodization recommends ([The Value of Post-Diet Maintenance](https://renaissanceperiodization.com/expert-advice/the-value-of-post-diet-maintenance)):
- Maximum of 12 weeks in a weight loss phase
- No more than 0.8% of bodyweight lost per week
- Spend an equivalent amount of time in a maintenance phase afterward

This minimizes muscle loss and maximizes health, adherence, and performance.

### Step 8: Expect to Be Hungry
Hunger is normal when eating at a calorie deficit. You may be able to reduce hunger through changes in *what* you eat, but you may not. Accept it as part of the process. For tips, see: [Managing Hunger While Losing Weight](https://thefitness.wiki/resources/community-campfires/#Managing_Hunger_While_Losing_Weight).

## Key Concepts

- **Calorie deficit** = eating below your TDEE → weight loss
- **Calorie surplus** = eating above your TDEE → weight gain
- **TDEE** = Total Daily Energy Expenditure (all calories your body burns in a day)
- Weight loss rate: aim for ~0.8% of bodyweight per week maximum
- The "best" diet is whichever one you can stick to consistently
