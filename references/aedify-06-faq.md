## Should I Bulk or Cut?

### Definitions
- **Bulk**: Deliberately eating a caloric surplus to gain weight over time
- **Cut**: Deliberately eating a caloric deficit to lose weight over time

The magnitude of surplus/deficit is not implied — any deliberate surplus is a "bulk" and any deliberate deficit is a "cut."

### Key Principle
"To improve body composition in the long term, you must forget about body composition in the short term." — John Berardi, PhD ([Tailor-Made Nutrition](https://www.t-nation.com/diet-fat-loss/tailor-made-nutrition-1))

See also MythicalStrength's alternate perspective: [Don't Cut Unless You Are Bodybuilding / Don't Bulk Unless It's the 1940s](http://mythicalstrength.blogspot.com/2016/04/dont-cut-unles-you-are-bodybuildingdont.html).

For personalized input, post photos at [r/BulkOrCut](http://old.reddit.com/r/bulkorcut) or in [r/Fitness Physique Phriday threads](https://www.reddit.com/r/Fitness/search?q=flair%3Aphysique&sort=new&restrict_sr=on).

### Decision Guide

**If clearly overweight** (fat, chubby, pudgy): Focus on losing weight. You'll get the best results in appearance, health, and how you feel. If you start resistance training, you may gain a small amount of muscle during weight loss.

**If clearly underweight** (thin, skinny): Focus on gaining weight, mostly as muscle. Eat in a surplus, get enough protein, do resistance training. Expect to gain some fat — that's OK. You can lose it later, and a modest amount of fat can make you look bigger without sacrificing much definition.

**If "skinnyfat"** (low muscle, high fat, normal weight): Focus on gaining muscle. Eat a modest surplus, get enough protein, do resistance training. Cutting is usually not recommended — you likely have too little muscle to look good lean, and a deficit will make training progress harder.

**If you can't decide which to do first**: It matters very little. Choose based on personal preference — would you rather temporarily be a bit fatter or a bit skinnier? The idea that you must get lean before bulking for better muscle-to-fat gain ratio is [not supported by evidence](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/p-ratios/).

### What About Recomposition (Losing Fat + Gaining Muscle Simultaneously)?

While [research has shown that it is technically possible](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/can-i-lose-fat-and-build-muscle-at-the-same-time/), it is generally not recommended because:
1. Diet margin for error is much lower — you must be very precise
2. It takes longer to see results than focusing on one goal
3. Progress is harder to measure and confirm
4. Performance and recovery may suffer without a surplus

Recomp is more feasible for: overly-fat and under-trained individuals, true beginners, and those returning after a long break. It becomes less viable the leaner and more trained you become.

**Recomp calorie target**: TDEE minus (bodyweight in lbs × 3.2 kcal) for ~0.7% bodyweight loss per week, which [research suggests](http://www.researchgate.net/publication/51113664_Effect_of_two_different_weight-loss_rates_on_body_composition_and_strength_and_power-related_performance_in_elite_athletes) can allow some muscle gain. See also: [Adding Muscle While Losing Fat – Q&A (Lyle McDonald)](https://web.archive.org/web/20110101210126/https://bodyrecomposition.com/fat-loss/adding-muscle-while-losing-fat-qa.html/)

## Why Can't I Lose Weight?

If you are not losing weight, you are not in a caloric deficit. Common reasons:

- Not tracking calories accurately (eyeballing portions, forgetting drinks/sauces/oils)
- Using inaccurate TDEE estimates
- Being inconsistent (eating well on weekdays but overeating on weekends)
- Counting exercise calories and eating them back (exercise calorie estimates are wildly inflated)

**The scale doesn't lie about energy balance.** Your body cannot maintain or gain weight in a true deficit — that would violate the laws of physics. [This 1992 study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/1454084) demonstrates that subjects who had difficulty losing weight did not have slow metabolisms — they wildly under-reported calories eaten and over-estimated activity levels.

## Why Can't I Gain Weight?

The mirror image of losing weight: you are not actually eating in a surplus. Common issues:

- Overestimating how much you eat
- Eating large meals infrequently but not enough total calories
- Not tracking calorie intake

If you're not gaining weight, you need to eat more. Track everything you consume. See: [Why can't I gain weight?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/why-cant-i-gain-weight/)

## How Quickly Can I Change My Body and Grow Muscle?

Realistic expectations for muscle gain (natural trainees, doing everything right):

- **Year 1**: ~20 lbs of muscle (less than 0.5 lb/week)
- **Year 2**: ~10 lbs
- **Year 3**: ~5 lbs
- Beyond: Diminishing returns

Don't expect visual changes for at least 1-2 months. Noticeable changes typically take 2-3+ months. *(Source: [Stronger By Science – Data Based Targets](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/realistic-training-goals/))*

## What Is More Important — Calories or Macros?

**Calories are more important.** For weight change, total caloric intake relative to TDEE is what matters. After calories, protein is the most important macro. Fat and carb distribution is a matter of personal preference within your calorie budget.

## How Do I Choose the Right Routine?

Consider:
1. **Schedule**: How many days per week and how long per session?
2. **Equipment access**: Gym with barbells? Home with dumbbells? Bodyweight only?
3. **Goals**: Primarily strength? Muscle size? General fitness? Sport-specific?

Don't overthink it — all [recommended routines](https://thefitness.wiki/routines/) work. Consistency matters more than routine selection. See also: [How do I choose the right routine for my goals?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/how-do-i-choose-the-right-routine-for-my-goals/)

## Can I Lose Fat in a Specific Body Part? (Spot Reduction)

**No.** Spot reduction is a myth. You cannot target where your body loses fat through exercise. Fat loss occurs across the entire body, determined by genetics and hormones. To lose fat in a specific area, you must reduce your overall body fat percentage through caloric deficit. See: [What exercises can I do to lose fat in my body part?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/what-exercises-can-i-do-to-lose-fat-in-my-body-part/)

## How Do I Get a Six Pack / Toned Abs?

Everyone has abdominal muscles. A visible six pack is a function of:
1. **Low body fat percentage** — the abs become visible when body fat is low enough
2. **Developed abdominal muscles** — building the muscles makes them more prominent

The primary path is reducing body fat through diet (caloric deficit). Ab exercises help develop the muscles but do NOT burn belly fat specifically. See: [How can I get a six pack / flat stomach / toned abs?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/how-can-i-get-a-six-pack-flat-stomach-toned-abs/) and [How do I get more toned?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/how-do-i-get-more-toned-how-do-i-tone-my-bodypart/)

## Should I Lift Weights If I'm Trying to Lose Weight?

**Yes, absolutely.** Resistance training while losing weight helps:
- Preserve existing muscle mass
- Potentially gain some muscle (especially as a beginner)
- Improve body composition
- Increase metabolic rate

Weight on the scale may not drop as fast, but you'll look and feel significantly better. See: [Should I lift weights if I am trying to lose weight?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/should-i-lift-weights-if-i-am-trying-to-lose-weight/)

## Does Cardio Kill Gains?

**No.** This is largely a myth. Cardio can actually improve your strength training by:
- Improving recovery between sets and sessions
- Supporting heart and lung health
- Enhancing work capacity

Excessive endurance training may interfere with muscle gains, but moderate cardio alongside lifting is beneficial. See: [Does cardio impair muscle gains?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/does-cardio-impair-muscle-gains/), [Cardio isn't going to kill your gains (SBS)](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/cardio-hypertrophy/), [Avoiding Cardio Could Be Holding You Back (SBS)](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/avoiding-cardio-could-be-holding-you-back/)

## What Rep Range Is Best for Muscle Growth?

Research shows muscle can be built effectively across a wide range of repetitions (roughly 5-30 reps per set), as long as the sets are taken close to failure. The old idea of specific "hypertrophy," "strength," and "endurance" rep ranges is [outdated and oversimplified](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/what-are-the-different-repetition-ranges-good-for/). Use a variety of rep ranges for best results. See: [What is the best rep range for muscle growth?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/what-is-the-best-rep-range-for-muscle-growth-hypertrophy/)

## Should I Always Lift to Failure?

Not necessarily. Training to failure is not required for muscle or strength gains and can increase injury risk and impair recovery. Most experts recommend stopping 1-3 reps short of failure (often called "Reps in Reserve" or RIR). Occasional sets to failure can be beneficial, but it shouldn't be the default for every set. See: [Should I always lift to failure?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/should-i-always-lift-to-failure/)

## Is Soreness a Good Indicator of a Good Workout?

**No.** Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (DOMS) is not a reliable indicator of workout quality or muscle growth. You can have an excellent, productive workout without feeling sore afterward. DOMS is most common when doing new exercises or after extended breaks. See: [Should I workout again if I'm still sore?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/should-i-workout-again-if-im-still-sore/) and [I'm not sore after my workout. Did I do something wrong?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/im-not-sore-after-my-workout-did-i-do-something-wrong/)

## Am I Overtraining?

Probably not. True overtraining is rare and typically only seen in elite athletes with extreme training loads. What most people experience is **under-recovery** — not eating enough, not sleeping enough, or dealing with excessive life stress. Address recovery factors before reducing training. See: [Am I overtraining?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/am-i-overtraining/)

---

## Did I Waste My "Noob Gains"?

**No.** Beginner gains are not a finite resource that can be "used up" or "wasted." The accelerated rate of progress experienced by beginners is available to anyone who is undertrained relative to their potential. If you took a break or didn't train optimally, you can still make rapid progress when you start training properly.

## Should I Train Full-Body or a Body-Part Split?

Both approaches are effective. Key considerations:
- **Full-body**: Better for 2-3 days per week, hits each muscle more frequently
- **Upper/Lower splits**: Good for 4 days per week
- **Push/Pull/Legs**: Good for 6 days per week (each workout done twice)

Research suggests training each muscle group at least twice per week may be slightly superior for growth. See: [Should I train full-body or a body-part split?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/should-i-train-full-body-or-a-body-part-split/)

## How Can I Get Motivated?

Motivation is unreliable — it comes and goes. Build **discipline** and **habits** instead. Treat fitness like brushing your teeth: something you do regardless of how you feel about it.

Practical tips:
- Start with something small and manageable
- Schedule your workouts like appointments
- Find activities you enjoy
- Track your progress for satisfaction
- Don't rely on feeling like it — just show up

See: [How can I get motivated to pursue fitness?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/how-can-i-get-motivated-to-pursue-fitness/) and [I don't enjoy exercise. How can I learn to love it?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/i-dont-enjoy-exercise-cardio-lifting-how-can-i-learn-to-love-it/)

## Is My Metabolism Super Fast or Really Slow?

Metabolic variation between individuals is much smaller than most people think. Research shows that the vast majority of people fall within a relatively narrow range of metabolic rates. "Fast" or "slow" metabolism is rarely the actual explanation for difficulty losing or gaining weight — it's almost always a matter of calorie intake. See: [Is my metabolism super fast/really slow?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/is-my-metabolism-super-fast-really-slow/)

## How Important Is Meal Timing?

For most people, **meal timing is not very important.** Total daily intake of calories and protein matters far more than when you eat them. Distributing protein across 3-4 meals may offer a small benefit for muscle growth, but this is a minor optimization compared to the big-picture factors. See: [How important is nutrient or meal timing?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/how-important-is-nutrient-or-meal-timing/)

## Can Exercise Help With Depression?

**Yes.** Exercise has been shown in research to have meaningful positive effects on depression and mood. Both cardiovascular exercise and resistance training appear beneficial. However, exercise is a complement to, not a replacement for, professional mental health treatment when needed. See: [Can exercise help with depression or generally improve my mood?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/can-exercise-help-with-depression-or-generally-improve-my-mood/)
