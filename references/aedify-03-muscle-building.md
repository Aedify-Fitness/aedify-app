# Muscle Building 101

> Source: thefitness.wiki — The official wiki of r/Fitness and r/weightroom on Reddit.

## The Three Pillars of Muscle Building

Building muscle comes down to three factors:

### 1. Resistance Training
- Resistance training is the stimulus that drives your body to build muscle
- Training must be difficult enough to drive growth and consistent over a long period
- Always follow a structured, proven routine created by a professional. Recommended routines: [Strength Training / Muscle Building](https://thefitness.wiki/routines/strength-training-muscle-building/)
- Without training stimulus, eating more will only make you fat
- Read more: [The Importance of Having a Proven Program](https://thefitness.wiki/adding-physical-activity/#The_Importance_of_Having_a_Proven_Program)

### 2. Calorie Surplus
- Eating more calories than your TDEE each day
- Necessary both to build muscle and to recover from training
- Start by increasing TDEE by 10-20% (TDEE × 1.1 to 1.2)
- Avoid going above 20% over TDEE — excess calories lead to excess fat, not more muscle

### 3. Protein
- To maximize muscle growth, aim for whichever is greater:
  - **160 grams per day**, OR
  - **0.8 grams per pound of bodyweight per day**
- Ideally spread across 3-4 meals throughout the day
- Minimum effective dose: ~120g/day
- Maximum shown beneficial for muscle growth: 0.82g/lb
- No danger in going above that amount (unless you eat *only* protein — see [protein poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_poisoning))

## Key Facts About Muscle Growth Rate

- Expect to gain at most ~2 lbs of muscle per month
- Don't expect visible changes for at least the first month
- Noticeable changes typically take 2-3 months minimum
- There are no shortcuts — marketers and charlatans want you to believe otherwise

*(Source: [Stronger By Science – Data Based Targets to Set Realistic Training Goals](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/realistic-training-goals/))*

## About Protein Sources

- Protein powders and mass gainers are simply powdered food — nothing special
- Whey protein has higher bioavailability than most other sources
- The primary advantage of shakes is **convenience** (easy to prepare, transport, store, consume)
- They are a tool to meet protein/calorie needs, not a requirement

If you're having trouble getting enough protein, see the [Eating a High Protein Diet](https://thefitness.wiki/resources/community-campfires/#Eating_a_High_Protein_Diet) community discussion.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Take "Before" Measurements
Weight and photos (front, back, side). Important for tracking progress since daily changes are hard to notice.

### Step 2: Estimate Your TDEE
Use any TDEE calculator. Treat it as an estimate only — expect to adjust as you gain weight.

### Step 3: Set a Daily Calorie Goal
Increase TDEE by 10-20%. More food does NOT always equal more muscle. Going too far above TDEE leads to:
- Increased fat gain
- Difficulty eating enough food without discomfort

### Step 4: Set a Daily Protein Goal
Minimum of 120g/day, ideally spread across four meals. Up to 0.82g/lb has been shown beneficial.

### Step 5: Track Calories and Protein
Use [MyFitnessPal](http://www.myfitnesspal.com/), [NutritionData](http://nutritiondata.self.com/), or the [nSuns Adaptive TDEE Spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1QTWDxFaB0r-7U6vZmG1s8of7jwd2GHHi/edit). Track everything. This step becomes essential if you're having trouble gaining weight. See also: [Why can't I gain weight?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/why-cant-i-gain-weight/)

### Step 6: Regular Progress Measurements
- Weigh yourself daily or at least weekly (unclothed, empty stomach)
- Track the trend, not daily fluctuations
- Monthly progress photos
- Avoid bio-electrical impedance body fat measurements

### Step 7: Adjust Diet Over Time
As you gain weight, your TDEE goes up. You'll need to increase your calorie intake periodically to continue gaining.

### Step 8: Be Patient
Weight can be gained quickly, but actual muscle growth is slow. Don't get discouraged by the pace.

## Choosing a Routine

- "Many roads lead to Rome" — most sensible routines work well with commitment and time
- What matters most: working hard and consistently
- Always defer to existing, proven routines from experienced professionals
- Don't try to reinvent the wheel — at best you'll come up with something equivalent, more likely something worse

## Recommended Routine Progression

1. Start with the [r/Fitness Basic Beginner Routine](https://thefitness.wiki/routines/r-fitness-basic-beginner-routine/) (max 3 months)
2. Switch to [5/3/1 for Beginners](https://thefitness.wiki/routines/5-3-1-for-beginners/) or [GZCLP](https://thefitness.wiki/routines/gzclp/) (at least 6 months)
3. After 6+ months, explore other routines as desired. See the [Program Review Archive](https://thefitness.wiki/intermediate-advanced-resources/program-review-archive/) for user experiences.
