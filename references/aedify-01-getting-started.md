# Getting Started with Fitness

> Source: thefitness.wiki — The official wiki of r/Fitness and r/weightroom on Reddit.

## Before You Begin

### It's OK to Start Small

Consistency over time is king. Many people fail because they try to make too many changes at once, feel overwhelmed, and either never start or are too easily tripped up. [Alan Thrall has a great video on this subject](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQbzfOE2B_M), emphasizing that doing *anything* for your fitness with consistency is better than doing nothing.

If you feel overwhelmed by all the information, pick one or two simple things to add or change in your life and do those consistently. Examples:

- Drink an extra glass of water every morning
- Go to the gym once a week and do anything you feel like
- Eat more vegetables with one meal a day

As these become easier, build on them. It is better to start small and succeed than to start big and quit because you bit off more than you could chew.

### Change Takes Time — Be Patient

Beginners often have the misconception they can achieve their goals quickly. Keep in mind:

- **Healthy weight loss rate**: ~0.8% of current bodyweight per week. Chasing rapid weight loss leads to frustration and yo-yoing. Slow and steady significantly increases the likelihood of keeping it off long-term. For more, see: [Weight Loss 101](https://thefitness.wiki/weight-loss-101/)
- **Muscle gain rate**: Absolute beginners doing everything right can expect to gain at most ~20 lbs of muscle in their first year (less than half a pound per week), and that rate drops over time. For more, see: [Muscle Building 101](https://thefitness.wiki/muscle-building-101/)
- **Simultaneous fat loss and muscle gain** is very difficult, requires significantly more effort, and shows visible results much more slowly than focusing on one goal at a time. For more, see: [Should I bulk or cut?](https://thefitness.wiki/faq/should-i-bulk-or-cut/)

### Fitness Goals Are Made of Simple Components

Most fitness goals boil down to some proportion of these concepts:

1. Losing some amount of fat
2. Gaining some amount of muscle
3. Doing some amount of strength/resistance training
4. Doing some amount of cardio/conditioning work
5. Practicing and improving skill in specific movement patterns
6. Making dietary changes to support the above

The real differences between goals are in the *degree* you work on each of these things.

### Avoid Majoring in the Minors (The 80/20 Rule)

The most important factors in achieving fitness goals are:

1. Doing an appropriate amount of training at an appropriate difficulty
2. Having some form of progression over time in your training
3. Eating an appropriate amount of calories and protein
4. Getting enough sleep, rest, and recovery from training
5. Consistency over time in all of the above

Every other factor will have significantly less impact on success. [The 80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle) is very much in play. Do not let pursuit of "perfect" be the enemy of achieving "good." Do not fall into the trap of premature optimization or [bikeshedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_triviality).

## The Two Major Components

Achieving fitness goals comes down to two pillars:

1. **[Improving Your Diet](https://thefitness.wiki/improving-your-diet/)** — managing calorie intake, protein, and overall food quality
2. **[Adding Physical Activity](https://thefitness.wiki/adding-physical-activity/)** — following a structured training program with appropriate progression
